How to shoot photos
for virtual staging

Great virtual staging starts with your photo. Empty rooms, shot clean and level, let our designers place furniture that looks like it was always there. Here is exactly how to capture rooms that stage perfectly, with a phone or a DSLR.

A 7-minute read · send it to whoever shoots the rooms
01 · Empty the room

The emptier the room, the more we can do

We furnish what the camera sees. A truly empty room gives our designers a blank canvas and the cleanest result.

  • Clear everything. Furniture, rugs, boxes, cords, ladders, cleaning gear, wall art. Even one leftover chair limits where we can place a sofa.
  • Keep the floor and corners clear. That is where the furniture lands, so nothing should sit on it.
  • Occupied home? Still fine. A tidy, decluttered room works too. Furniture removal is included, so we can digitally clear existing pieces, but an empty room always stages cleanest.
  • Wipe the lens and clean the glass. Smudges and hazy lenses are the most common cause of soft photos.
One clean, empty angle beats five busy ones. We stage what is in the frame, so a single well-shot room is worth more than a dozen cluttered snaps.
02 · Frame the whole room

Floor to ceiling, into a corner

A sofa needs floor to sit on. If the floor is cropped, there is nowhere to place furniture believably.

  • Back into the doorway and capture the full room: plenty of floor, the walls, and a little ceiling.
  • Shoot into a corner, a two-wall angle, not flat at a single wall. It shows depth and gives us a natural place to build a room.
  • Leave generous floor in the frame, especially the foreground. That open floor is where the main furniture goes.
  • One hero angle per room is enough. For large or open-plan spaces, a second angle helps us zone it.
03 · Keep it level and straight

Straight lines make the furniture look real

  • Hold the camera at chest height (about 4 to 4.5 ft) and keep it level. High or low angles distort the floor, and furniture ends up sitting wrong.
  • Turn on the grid and keep vertical lines truly vertical. Door frames, wall edges and corners should run straight up and down, not lean.
  • Stay consistent room to room. The same height and level across shots keeps the whole set looking like one shoot.
Perspective matters more here than anywhere. If the walls lean in your photo, the furniture we add leans too.
04 · Light it bright and even

Even light lets us match shadows

Added furniture casts its own shadow. It only looks real when the room's light is soft and consistent.

  • Open it up. Blinds up, curtains back, every light on. Bright, even rooms stage best.
  • Avoid harsh hotspots and deep black shadows. Mid-morning or a bright-overcast day is ideal; harsh noon sun throws patches we have to fight.
  • Do not shoot into a window. Keep the main window to your side so the room lights evenly instead of going dark against the glare.
  • Kill the flash. On-phone flash flattens the room and blows out the nearest wall.
05 · Wide, but never warped

Show the space without bending it

  • Wide enough to show the whole room, but skip the extreme ultra-wide or fisheye. It curves the floor and walls, and furniture placed on a warped floor never looks right.
  • On a phone, use the main 1x lens (0.7x at most). Avoid the widest 0.5x setting.
  • HDR on or auto. Tap to focus on a mid-tone, then slide exposure down until the window stops glowing.
  • Brace or use a tripod. Blur cannot be fixed, and it makes clean staging impossible.
06 · Send-ready files

How to hand them over

  • Highest resolution, landscape (horizontal) orientation.
  • JPG or PNG, straight from the camera. Light straightening is fine. Skip heavy filters, HDR halos and ultra-wide correction so we start from a clean image.
  • No people, pets, or reflections of you in mirrors or windows. We cannot stage a room with someone in it.
  • One clean angle per room, ordered front to back so nothing gets missed.

Do

  • Empty the room fully
  • Keep the whole floor in frame
  • Shoot level, into a corner
  • Bright, soft, even light

Don't

  • Leave furniture or clutter
  • Crop the floor tight
  • Tilt or shoot ultra-wide
  • Use heavy filters or flash
